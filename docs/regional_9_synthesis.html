Reading the grid. The chain is right-sized (init=600 ≈ 6 wks of demand = total LT+1), so the planner reaches steady-state quickly and the supplier orders match weekly demand. Differences across the 9 cells come from where the 600 units sit at week 0 and how skewed the regional demand is.

Best margin: 70/30, dist 30/30/40 → margin €+4,705 (+18.2% of revenue), sell-through 81.1%, service 99.3%.
Best sell-through: 50/50, dist 0/30/70 → 81.1%.
Worst margin: 90/10, dist 0/30/70 → margin €+1,815.

Stock distributions trade off the same way for every split: 100% at stores maximises sell-through and margin (no idle units upstream), but asks every store to be right-sized from W0; the 30/30/40 spread keeps margin positive but always trails 0/0/100 because upstream units sit a week longer before reaching shelves. Region-split skew (50/50 → 90/10) barely moves margin when the chain is right-sized — the planner discovers the share at the first review and re-balances allocations, so a 90/10 split with smart distribution burns through Region A's stores faster but the chain compensates.
