9 Regional Scenarios — 13-week chain

Two-region warehouse model. Grid: 3 demand splits × 3 stock distributions.
Total chain LT 13 wk Upstream: mat 6 + semi 3 + fp 2 = 11 wk Distribution: CW→RW 1 + RW→Store 1 = 2 wk Coverage 14 wk Init 1,400 units (= LT+1) 50 stores / region Flat 100/wk × 26 wks No prod_cap Smart distrib

Hypotheses fixed across the 9 cells

  • Total demand identical: 100 pcs/wk × 26 wks = 2,600 units in every cell. The split slider only changes how those 2,600 are divided between Region A and B; the chain sees the same aggregate signal.
  • Right-sized chain: initial stock equals (total LT + 1) × demand. The planner reaches steady state quickly and the supplier orders track weekly demand 1-for-1 (~100 pcs/wk).
  • Steady-state production is the same in every cell: produced ≈ 2,600 + WIP ≈ init ≈3,900 units regardless of split or distribution. That's why sell-through clusters tightly around 66–67% — the differences come entirely from the small fraction of demand that ends up missed.
  • Margin differences are missed-sales differences: each missed unit = −€10 revenue at unchanged COGS. With 99% service rates, the spread is naturally narrow.
  • Planner discovers the regional split at the first review (week 1, since freq=1). It is locked from then on and never re-bases.
  • Smart distribution is on everywhere: water-fills both CW→RW (prioritise starved region) and RW→Store (within region only — no cross-region transfers).
  • No production cap: the chain produces freely. Tightening prod_cap or shrinking initial stock are the levers that would amplify differences between distributions.

Why 0/0/100 margins look similar across splits: total demand is fixed at 2,600 and the chain produces at steady state ~100/wk regardless of split, so the only thing that varies is where the missed units land. At 50/50 the load is symmetric and nobody starves. At 70/30 and 90/10, Region B is starved at its stores from W0, but the chain has 13 weeks to refill it — with smart distribution catching the imbalance at the first review, most of the gap is recovered before the run ends. The 30/30/40 distribution wins at every skew because the CW + RW stock gives the smart allocator material to redirect once it knows the split.

Best margin: 70/30 · 30 / 30 / 40 → €+715 (+2.8%).
Worst margin: 90/10 · 0 / 0 / 100 → €+540.
Spread across the 9 cells: €175. Modest in absolute terms, but Region B's service rate falls as low as 82.7% on the worst cell — a meaningful retail KPI even when total margin barely moves.
