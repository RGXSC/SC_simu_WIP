9 Regional Scenarios — One-shot product (no replenishment)

The chain produces nothing after week 0. The 2,600 units of total demand can only be served from initial stock.
Production cap 2,600 = init (no supplier orders) Total LT 5 wk (Agile) Flat 100/wk × 26 wks → total demand 2,600 50 stores / region Smart distribution on Price €10/u · VC €5/u · Fixed €5,200

Hypotheses fixed across the 9 cells

  • CORRECTED W0 stock split 50/50 between regions: at W0 the operator does NOT yet know the demand split — the planner only discovers it at the first review. So both RW init and store init are divided evenly between Region A and Region B regardless of the slider's ground truth. This is the lever that exposes A-side starvation at skewed splits: 90/10 means A faces 2,340 demand with only 1,300 units of dedicated W0 stock.
  • NEW One-shot product: the lifetime production cap is set to 2,600 = initial stock. Supplier orders are forced to zero from W1 onwards. Whatever the chain holds at week 0 is all it will ever have.
  • NEW Total LT shortened to 5 wk (Agile): upstream LT is dead weight here (nothing is produced), so we leave only the distribution legs (CW→RW=1, RW→Store=1) plus a token mat/semi/fp=1 each.
  • Total demand identical: 100 pcs/wk × 26 wks = 2,600 units in every cell. The split slider only changes how those 2,600 are divided between Region A and B.
  • Smart distribution is on everywhere: water-fills CW→RW (prioritise starved region) and RW→Store (within region, no cross-region transfers). Once the planner discovers the split, it routes the CW pool to whoever is short — but it cannot reroute units already at stores or already in an RW.
  • Strict regional isolation: stock at RW A only feeds Region A's stores; same for B. So if Region B has surplus, it can't rescue Region A.
  • Planner discovers the regional split at the first review (week 1, since freq=1). It is locked from then on and never re-bases.
